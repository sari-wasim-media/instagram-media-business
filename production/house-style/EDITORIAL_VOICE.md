# Editorial voice — how the desk speaks

**Status: ACTIVE — delegated production decision (2026-09-08).** Implements the founders' brand-posture
decision of the same date (`DECISIONS.md`): during the controlled experiment both accounts operate as a
**faceless, opinionated editorial media desk** — not a founder-personality brand, not a neutral repost page,
not a news aggregator. One voice on both accounts, because the experiment measures accounts, engines and
formats, not two personalities. The frozen profile variables (bios, display names, photos) are untouched.

## 1. The posture in one paragraph

The desk has opinions and shows its work. It ranks, it rules, it reads the market, and it says what it thinks
in plain declarative sentences with the facts on the frame. It is nobody in particular: no "I", no founder
names, no face, no personal anecdotes. Authority comes from consistency, sources and the willingness to call a
release a PASS.

## 2. Voice rules

| Rule | Do | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Declarative** | "Buy at retail. Do not pay resale." | "You might want to consider…" · "In our humble opinion…" |
| **Specific** | "$185, sizes US 7–14, app raffle Saturday." | "Dropping soon, pricey, good luck." |
| **Sourced** | Every number carries a source and a date on the frame or in the caption | A figure with no origin |
| **Opinionated, not abusive** | "The 2024 pair is the same shoe. Pass." | Insults, dunking on named people, brand-bashing by name |
| **Nominative** | Products named exactly as the brand writes them, to identify what is being discussed | Implying partnership, access, sponsorship, "sent to us", "official" |
| **Desk pronouns** | "The desk", "this list", plain statements with no subject | "I", "we think" as persons, founder names |
| **Sentence case, no exclamation marks, at most one emoji per caption, never at the start** | | ALL-CAPS captions, emoji strings |
| **No asks** | Let the summary slide earn the save | "Save this", "tag a friend", "comment your size", "follow for more" |
| **No commerce** | No links (locked), no codes, no retailer pushes, no "cop at [store]" | Affiliate language of any kind |
| **American spelling**, one house style on both accounts | "color", "favorite" | Mixed spellings |

## 3. Per-engine register

- **C1 Release Desk** — the newsroom voice: facts first, verdict second, reasons third. Verdicts are COP ·
  WAIT · PASS and nothing else. Rulings are one sentence.
- **C2 Rankings** — the critic's voice: a reason line per item is one argument, ≤ 60 characters, present
  tense. The basis of the ranking is always stated. "Ranked by looks-per-dollar" beats "our favorites".
- **C3 Market Desk** — the analyst's voice: findings, not questions; "snapshot" on every frame, "not advice"
  in every caption; direction signs and absolute values behind every percentage; no predictions.
- **C4 Commentary** — the columnist's voice: one observation about the culture, the joke in the writing, the
  image as the straight man. Roast the behaviour, never a named person or a brand by name.
- **Stories** — the desk's margin notes: shorter, same rules. Thursday's DESK NOTES is where corrections and
  method live, and where the desk says plainly that no list carries a sponsor during this run.

## 4. What the desk says about itself, and where

The bios are frozen, so the posture is carried by the content: the engine label in every top band, the
sources on every frame, the verdict stamps, and the recurring DESK NOTES line — "how we rank: three tests,
no sponsors in any list during this run, corrections posted here." Nothing else identifies the desk, and
nothing identifies the founders.

## 5. Why one voice on two accounts

Two voices would be a second experimental variable correlated with account. The locked anti-duplication rules
already require the identical house template on both accounts; the voice is part of that template. Distinct
account identities are a post-experiment question (`ROADMAP.md`, "Later").
