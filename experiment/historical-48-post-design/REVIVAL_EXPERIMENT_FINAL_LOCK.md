## 3. Final experiment duration

**6 posting weeks, 2-week measurement tail, decision at end of week 8. Plus a week 0 for setup.**

Not chosen by inheritance. Four constraints determine it:

1. **The 30-day clock.** Recommendation eligibility recovers only after 30 clear days since the last unoriginal post. A fair read of "can this account get recommended with original content" requires ≥30 consecutive days of original posting. Six weeks (42 days) gives 30 days plus buffer for the clock to have fully run before the primary measurement window.
2. **Posts per cell.** 4 posts/week × 6 weeks = 24 per account, 6 per cell per account, 12 per cell pooled across accounts. Four weeks would give 8 pooled per cell, which is too thin to rank four cells against heavy-tailed view distributions.
3. **Maturation.** Reels accumulate for weeks. Every post is snapshotted at a **fixed age of T+7 days**, Reels also at **T+14**. Fixed maturation age is the methodological point: never compare a week-1 post at 40 days old to a week-6 post at 5 days old.
4. **Dormancy warm-up.** Weeks 1–2 on a three-year-dormant account are not representative of steady state.

**Analysis windows, which differ by question:**

| Question Window n          |                                      |                  |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| Go / no-go on each account | Median of weeks 3–6                  | 16 posts/account |
| Cell ranking               | All 6 weeks, pooled across accounts  | 12 posts/cell    |
| Warm-up detection          | Weeks 1–2 median vs weeks 3–6 median | —                |

**Adaptive, but only in a bounded way.** The matrix is frozen through week 4. Weeks 5–6 permit two specific adaptations defined in §13. There is a genuine decision gate at end of week 4, not just at week 8.

**Honest limitation, stated up front:** with 6 posts per cell per account, this experiment **ranks; it does not prove.** Only differences of roughly 2x or larger will be readable above noise. It also measures the *median*, not the *tail* — and theme-page economics are tail-driven, as the 2.6M Reel demonstrates. A 6-week test at this volume has a low probability of catching a rare breakout. Do not read the absence of a viral hit as evidence that breakouts are impossible.

## 6. Two-account experimental design

**Answer to your question: same four cells, same format allocation, same cadence — different topics and different assets.** Not differentiated editorial roles.

**Why not differentiated roles:** editorial roles are an *output* of this experiment, not an input. If you assign A "news" and B "memes" now, and A wins, you cannot tell whether A is the better account or news is the better cell. You would have destroyed the account effect to save a little production effort.

**Anti-duplication rules** (required, because near-identical detection judges the file and posting the same asset across accounts is a negative signal):

1. Never the same file on both accounts.
2. Never the same specific subject on both accounts within 14 days.
3. Both accounts draw from a shared topic backlog; a topic is checked out to one account and locked for 14 days.
4. Different source imagery for every piece, even on related subjects.

**Sequence counterbalancing:** Account A runs the weekly cell×format schedule in order, weeks 1→6. **Account B runs the identical matrix in reverse week order.** This decouples week number from cell×format across the two accounts, so an external shock in a given week (a major drop, a platform anomaly) doesn't hit the same cell in the same format on both accounts.

**How the three effects get separated:**

| Effect Comparison n            |                                                                         |             |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| **Account**                    | A's 24 posts vs B's 24 posts, on identical cell/format structure        | 24 vs 24    |
| **Format**                     | 24 Reels vs 12 carousels vs 12 static, pooled across accounts and cells | 24/12/12    |
| **Cell**                       | 12 posts per cell, pooled across accounts, format-balanced              | 12 per cell |
| **Account × cell interaction** | Is the cell ranking the same on both accounts?                          | —           |

That last row is the guard against a false conclusion. **If a cell only dominates on one account, it is not an engine effect — it is an account-specific result and must not be generalised into Phase 3.**

**One expected asymmetry to read carefully:** B has never published a Reel. Do not over-interpret weak B Reels performance in weeks 1–3; give B's Reels the weeks 3–6 window specifically.

## 8. Legacy-content pre-test decision

**Recommendation: inspect individually now, remove the narrow counterfeit-promotion subset before post #1, leave everything else untouched.**

This is not mass deletion. Based on your evidence pack it is likely a handful of posts.

**Why not "leave and clean later":**

- The removal cost is effectively zero. These posts collectively generate \~9–12 views a day across an entire account. You are giving up nothing measurable.
- The risk is complaint-driven and asymmetric. Meta removes or restricts content that promotes suspected counterfeit goods or links off-platform to counterfeit sites, and repeated infringement can result in the account being disabled. Green today does not mean safe tomorrow — it means nobody has reported it yet.
- **Experimental contamination is the decisive argument.** Any account-state change must happen before post #1, never mid-measurement. "Clean later" means cleaning during the test.

**Why removal rather than archiving for this category:** archiving hides content from the public grid but the content remains on the platform under the account. For IP exposure specifically, removal is the action that eliminates it. Archiving is the right tool for merely-embarrassing content, not for this.

**Why NOT to mass-delete anything else:** the originality assessment only looks at recent posts within a rolling 30-day window. Posts from 2019–2021 have no effect whatsoever on recommendation eligibility. Deleting them destroys engagement history and social proof to fix a problem that doesn't exist.

**Removal criteria — both conditions must hold:**

1. The post promotes a third-party seller (branded creative, seller name, or a CTA/link to a shop), **and**
2. It shows trademarked goods at prices materially below authenticated-market levels, **or** names a seller using replica-market terminology.

**Before removing each post:** screenshot the post *and* its Insights. This preserves the analytics record you were concerned about losing.

**Also check for the same category in:** Story Highlights, and the current bio link destination.

**Leave untouched:** memes, collections, colorway grids, celebrity fits, reposted images carrying third-party watermarks, and promotions for advertisers selling legitimate goods.

**On the old "permanent post" promises:** these are five-plus years old and, in this specific subset, are commitments to promote counterfeit goods. Note it and move on.

## 11. Posting-time protocol

**Freeze it. One fixed slot per account. Do not test time during this experiment.**

Instagram's "most active times" graph is computed from actual follower activity and is therefore already geography-weighted. It is the correct instrument, and second-guessing it with manual timezone arithmetic would be substituting a worse estimate for a better one. Your observed 18:00–21:00 GMT+3 window maps to 15:00–18:00 UTC, 11:00–14:00 ET, 08:00–11:00 PT — which sits reasonably against the US share on both accounts.

| Account A Account B      |             |             |
| ------------------------ | ----------- | ----------- |
| Feed posts (all formats) | 19:00 GMT+3 | 19:45 GMT+3 |
| Stories                  | 20:15 GMT+3 | 21:00 GMT+3 |

**On the 45-minute offset:** the two audiences are described as broadly similar and likely overlap. Posting simultaneously would make the accounts compete for the same attention in the same minute. The offset is a small, *constant* confound accepted to avoid a larger variable one — and because it is constant, it cannot explain any within-account variation.

**Why time is not a test factor:** with 6 posts per cell, adding a time dimension would fragment the design into cells of 3 and make everything unreadable. Time optimisation is a follow-up experiment for after week 8, run on the winning account with the winning cell.

## 13. Early-stop and adaptation rules

**Governing principle: the matrix is frozen weeks 1–4. Adaptation is permitted only in weeks 5–6.** This guarantees at least 4 posts per cell per account under identical conditions.

### Hard stop — any time

- **Account Status changes from green in any category on either account.** Pause that account immediately. Screenshot everything, including the specific flagged item and the exact guideline named. Request review if offered. Continue the other account; the paused account's data series ends at that date.
- **Any content removal notice or IP report.** Pause both accounts, investigate before resuming.

### Investigate — but keep posting

- **Zero non-follower reach on all Reels for two consecutive weeks on one account.** Verify no unoriginal asset slipped in; verify Reels are 30–60 seconds, which produced the highest reach rate at 5.60% in Socialinsider's 2026 analysis, and that none exceed 3 minutes since those are not recommended to non-followers at all; check Account Status.
- **One account's reach exceeds the other's by >3x for two consecutive weeks.** Note it. Do not act. This is the account effect being measured, not a problem to fix.

### Double down — weeks 5–6 only

If one cell's pooled median on its primary metric is ≥2x the next-best cell after week 4, that cell may take **one extra Reel slot per account per week**, taken from the lowest-performing cell. Log the change. Analyse weeks 1–4 and weeks 5–6 separately in the final read.

### Drop a cell — weeks 5–6 only

If a cell's pooled median reach rate is <40% of the account's overall median after week 4, drop it for weeks 5–6.

### Extend

- **Production slipped below 80% of plan** → extend posting to week 8 rather than analysing an incomplete matrix. An incomplete matrix is worse than a delayed one.
- **A post exceeds 50x the running median reach in weeks 5–6** → extend two weeks to test whether the format is repeatable and to measure the follower-retention tail. This is the one case where an outlier changes the plan, and it changes the *schedule*, not the verdict.

### Never do, mid-test

- **Do not move effort between accounts.** This destroys the account comparison, which is one of the three things the experiment exists to measure. Reallocation is a week 8 decision.
- Do not change posting time, display name, bio, profile photo, or pinned posts.
- Do not add a fifth cell.
- Do not post an unoriginal asset "just this once." That resets the 30-day clock and invalidates the eligibility read for the entire remaining test.

*Definition:* one cell's pooled median on its primary metric is ≥2x the next-best cell.

**Conclude:** content-market fit found. Short of Scenario A, this is the most valuable possible outcome.

Two honest options: (i) start clean pages with everything learned, seeding them by cross-posting to the old accounts, or (ii) stop. Do not spend twelve months reviving assets that failed two consecutive tests.
**Phase 3:** No. **Deprioritize:** Both. **Repositioning:** Yes, or exit.
