## 3. The strategic tensions that actually matter

### Tension 1: The historical model is not transferable, and it's not a small detail

You asked me not to run a forensic investigation into old advertisers, and I won't. But I cannot build a revenue plan on the historical menu without telling you what that menu was actually selling, because it determines whether any of your historical evidence predicts future demand.

The paid creatives price an Off-White x Jordan 1 Chicago at $250 against Farfetch's $10,000, a Balenciaga Triple S at $216 against $995, and an Audemars Piguet Royal Oak at $140. A real Royal Oak starts around $20,000. The second advertiser's name, "UA Supply," uses "UA" in its established replica-market sense. These are replica sellers. There is no ambiguity in the numbers.

Three consequences, none of them about the past:

**Your proven revenue channel is closed by your own rules.** You've excluded scams and deceptive promotions. Replica promotion is also explicitly prohibited by Meta, which states it removes or restricts content that promotes suspected counterfeit goods or links off-platform to counterfeit sites, and can disable the responsible account. That is the largest single threat to the asset you're trying to protect, and it's already in the feed as permanent posts you sold.

**Your historical pricing tells you almost nothing about legitimate demand.** $25 per post was replica-seller pricing, from buyers who had no other distribution channel and no compliance function. Legitimate brands price differently, buy differently, and do due diligence. Treat the old menu as evidence about a market you are leaving, not a floor for the market you are entering.

**Part of your follower base was assembled by an offer you can no longer make.** Some proportion of those 44.8k followed for cheap fakes. That segment does not convert for a real retailer, which suppresses the CPM you can defend.

My recommendation is that this category is permanently closed, and I'll design accordingly. But I need you to confirm it explicitly rather than let it stay ambiguous, because if any part of you is planning to take that money again when cash is tight, the entire architecture below is the wrong one.

### Tension 2: Your growth engine is now structurally penalized

The organic images are reposts. The Jordan 1 colorway grid carries `@supremedunks`' watermark. The celebrity fit breakdown carries another page's mark. The collection rooms are other people's photos. This was a standard 2019–2021 aggregator operation, and it worked because the algorithm rewarded it.

It doesn't anymore. Instagram now excludes accounts that primarily post unoriginal photos, videos, or carousels from recommendation surfaces entirely, judged at the account level over a rolling window. Adding watermarks, changing speed, or reposting a screenshot with visible credit does not count as transformation. Existing followers still see your posts; you simply become undiscoverable to everyone else. Separately, near-duplicate detection means posting the same file across several of your own pages is a negative signal, which directly attacks the naive version of the network plan.

This is the hinge of the whole strategy. Discovery is the only path from 44.8k to 500k, and reposting removes you from discovery.

### Tension 3: Your two headline targets are coupled, and the follower target is the hard one

Rough arithmetic, all assumptions stated so you can attack them.

**Revenue.** $6,000/month at 8 retained clients is $750/client/month. To defend $750 you need to deliver roughly 40–70k impressions per client per month, since fair Instagram CPM benchmarks sit around $10–25 for lifestyle-adjacent audiences. If sponsored content stays at a trust-preserving \~20–25% of output, total network output needs to be roughly 2M impressions/month. That is demanding but not fantasy.

**Followers.** +455,200 net in twelve months. Reels convert viewers to followers at roughly 0.5–2%. That implies somewhere between 25M and 90M views over the year, call it 2–4M views/month averaged and 8–10M/month by month 12. At a realistic 20k average views per Reel, that's 100–200 original Reels per month sustained.

So: the revenue target is the more achievable of the two, and the follower target is the aggressive one. They are not independent goals you pursue in parallel. The reach required for $6k is a subset of the reach required for 500k. My working view is that **$6k/month is defensible and 500k followers is roughly 2–3x optimistic** on a 12-month organic horizon starting from dormancy, and I'd rather hit the revenue number with 180k followers than miss both chasing the vanity metric.

Both numbers also depend on a variable you haven't given me: audience geography. A US/UK/German audience supports $10–25 CPM. A predominantly South Asian, MENA, or Southeast Asian audience supports $1–4, and no amount of good strategy closes that gap. This single unknown moves the revenue model by a factor of five.

### Tension 4: The 10% automation target fights the growth target directly

100–200 *original* Reels per month is the requirement. Original content production is the least automatable, highest-judgment part of this business. The automatable version is reposting, which is precisely what now kills reach. You cannot have maximum automation and maximum discovery in 2026; the platform has priced that trade-off deliberately.

More bluntly: months 1–6 will need 20–30 combined hours per week, not 8. The 10% figure is a month-12 exit condition for a system you build with real labor first, not an operating constraint you apply from day one. If neither of you can commit real hours through month 6, the honest answer is that this plan doesn't work and we should design a much smaller one.

### Tension 5: Two small things with outsized commercial impact

`sneakerbong` contains "bong." To a brand-safety reviewer at any legitimate retailer, that reads as drug paraphernalia. It also contradicts your own content boundaries. That name is a tax on every enterprise-grade deal you'll ever try to close, and I'd treat renaming as near-mandatory rather than optional.

Second, you currently have no way to accept money from a real business. A retailer's finance department needs an invoice, an entity, and a payment rail that isn't a personal PayPal. This is a $0–100 problem that blocks 100% of the revenue plan, and it belongs in the first 30 days.
